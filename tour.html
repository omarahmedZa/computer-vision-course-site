What’s inside this course

A small, hands-on tour: read a concept, see it, try it in a lab, then check yourself.

1 · Lessons

There are 8 core lessons across 4 units. Each lesson follows a consistent structure so you always know where to find intuition, math, and practice:

SectionPurpose
Why this mattersMotivation and where the idea shows up in real vision systems.
The one core ideaThe central concept in plain language and notation.
How it actually worksStep-by-step mechanism, math/algorithm, and geometric/photometric intuition.
Worked example(s)Concrete numbers or cases you can trace by hand.
The trapCommon misconceptions and failure modes to watch for.
Retrieval checkShort questions with hidden answers for self-testing.
Labs & next stepsLinks to interactive demos and the next lesson.

2 · Figures and intuition

Lessons use diagrams, formulas, tables, and lightweight visuals to connect pixels → math → behavior. Many ideas are shown geometrically (coordinates, transforms, gradients, parameter spaces) or photometrically (intensity, histograms, blending).

TipWhen a figure is interactive, change one parameter at a time and predict the effect before you observe it.

3 · Companion labs

Companion lab pages provide self-contained, interactive demos (SVG/canvas/inline JavaScript, no external dependencies). They let you experiment with kernels, warping/interpolation, corner/blob responses, Hough voting, RANSAC, blending, registration, and CNN feature/receptive-field intuition.

4 · Progress and checks

You can mark section confidence and score retrieval questions locally in your browser. The dashboard shows progress and a Continue link; Export record saves a markdown summary to your device. Nothing is uploaded.

5 · How to study a lesson

  1. Read “Why this matters” and “The one core idea” without peeking at answers.
  2. Work through the steps and the worked example on paper where possible.
  3. Open the linked lab and vary inputs to build intuition.
  4. Answer the retrieval check, then reveal and grade honestly.
  5. Note the trap — it’s usually where real bugs and exam mistakes hide.

6 · Curriculum path

The course moves from image fundamentals and filtering → geometric transforms/warping → local features and robust fitting → blending/registration → deep learning for vision. See CURRICULUM.md for prerequisites and objectives.
