Lesson 04 — Feature Detectors/Descriptors (SIFT)

Scale-space extrema · keypoint localization/orientation · descriptor · invariances · matching

Pipeline

  • 1) Detect: DoG pyramid, find extrema in (x,y,σ) over 26 neighbors.
  • 2) Localize/filter: sub-pixel/scale refinement; reject low-contrast and edge-like (unstable) points.
  • 3) Orientation: gradient orientation histogram in local neighborhood → dominant peak = canonical orientation (rotation invariance); strong secondary peaks may duplicate keypoint.
  • 4) Descriptor: rotate to canonical frame, take ~16×16 region → 4×4 cells × 8 orientation bins = 128-D; weight by magnitude/Gaussian; normalize, clamp, renormalize.

Matching basics

  • Compare descriptors by distance (e.g. Euclidean/L2).
  • Ratio test: accept if d1/d2 < τ (≈0.7–0.8) — best much better than second-best (distinctive).
  • Geometric verification (e.g. RANSAC) removes remaining outliers.

Invariances

  • Scale: detect at characteristic σ / scale-normalized neighborhood.
  • Rotation: canonical orientation alignment.
  • Illumination (partial): gradients + normalization reduce additive/multiplicative effects.
  • Not fully invariant: large affine/viewpoint change, non-rigid deformation, occlusion, extreme blur/lighting.

Key formulas/ideas

D(x,y,σ)=L(x,y,kσ)-L(x,y,σ)
Descriptor: 4×4 spatial bins × 8 orientation bins = 128
Normalize → clamp large bins → normalize

Pitfalls

  • “Invariant to everything” — false; check assumptions.
  • Nearest neighbor alone is ambiguous in repetitive textures.
  • Many keypoints ≠ good matches (need distinctiveness/stability).
