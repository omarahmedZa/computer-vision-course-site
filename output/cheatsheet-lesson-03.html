Lesson 03 — Corner and Blob Detection

Features · gradients/structure · Harris corners · NMS · LoG/DoG scale-space blobs

Core ideas

  • Why features: repeatable, localizable points for matching, tracking, registration, stitching.
  • Local structure: flat (small gradients), edge (change in one direction), corner (change in two directions — aperture intuition).
  • Harris: second-moment/structure tensor M over window; eigenvalues λ1,λ2 indicate flat/edge/corner. Response R = det(M) − k·trace(M)^2, threshold + NMS.
  • Blobs: regions distinct from surroundings at some scale. Scale-space L(x,y,σ)=Gσ*I; detect extrema across space and scale.
  • LoG/DoG: normalized LoG (σ^2∇^2L) peaks at blob scale; DoG = L(x,y,kσ)−L(x,y,σ) approximates LoG efficiently.

Harris pipeline

  • Compute gradients Ix,Iy → build M (smoothed Ix^2, Iy^2, IxIy) → compute R → threshold → non-maximum suppression.

Key formulas

M = Σ w(x,y)[[Ix^2, IxIy],[IxIy, Iy^2]]
R = det(M) - k(trace(M))^2, k≈0.04–0.06
L(x,y,σ)=G(x,y,σ)*I
DoG = G_{kσ}*I - G_σ*I

Interpretation

  • Edge: one large, one small eigenvalue (R low/negative).
  • Corner: both large (R strongly positive).
  • Blob response strongest when filter scale ≈ blob size.

Pitfalls

  • High gradient magnitude ≠ corner (edges fail aperture test).
  • Threshold/window/smoothing sensitivity; clusters without NMS.
  • Single-scale detectors miss different-sized structures.
  • Contrast/illumination affects raw responses.
